# ASIOR scaling checklist

This checklist turns Shreyas's October 11, 2026 marketing plan into work that can be checked off. The Shopify figures below are historical baselines from August 28–September 27, 2026, not current performance. Refresh the data before using it in public or making decisions from it.

## 1. Confirm the foundation before scaling content

- [ ] Confirm whether the first two weeks of the September 27 kickoff work are complete; finish any open tasks before advancing the content test.
- [ ] Audit the complete purchase journey on both a phone and a laptop.
- [ ] Capture each checkout screen and record the displayed final cost and delivery estimate.
- [ ] Note where a shopper could hesitate, plus any checkout error observed. Treat these as findings to investigate, not assumed causes.
- [ ] If a real test order is needed, confirm reimbursement and exact instructions with Sathvik first.
- [ ] Send Sathvik a short, ranked list of store issues with screenshots for approval and implementation.
- [ ] Use current, verified facts for fit, sizing, materials, care, shipping, and returns. Ask Sathvik when a fact is not confirmed.
- [ ] Make each product easier to judge: show a real person wearing it, how it sits on the shoulders, how it moves while walking, fabric texture, stitching, and drape.
- [ ] Put fabric weight, care, or size details on screen only after verifying them.
- [ ] Keep product photography and video clear enough to assess the real garment; avoid effects that hide details.

## 2. Build genuine trust and customer feedback

- [ ] Draft a personal review request for the 13 customers whose orders were delivered; Sathvik sends it.
- [ ] Invite honest feedback, never positive-only reviews. Do not invent testimonials, offer a reward for a positive review, or quote a customer without permission.
- [ ] Send a straightforward post-delivery check-in about fit and whether the item arrived as expected, so ASIOR can learn before asking for another purchase.
- [ ] Ask after the customer has had time to experience the item.
- [ ] Send at most one polite review follow-up if the customer does not respond.
- [ ] Keep the review request and follow-up as drafts until Sathvik approves them.
- [ ] Use customer feedback to improve product information and service; do not imply that one small set of responses proves a broad trend.

Suggested review-request draft from the plan:

> Subject: Quick question about your ASIOR order
>
> Hey [first name], thanks again for ordering from ASIOR. I wanted to check how your piece has been fitting and whether everything arrived as expected. If you have a minute, an honest review would mean a lot to us as we improve the brand. Here’s the review link: [link]. And if anything was off, just reply and let us know. Thanks — ASIOR.

## 3. Run the two-week organic content test

- [ ] Batch several useful clips in one afternoon with free tools already available; plan and submit them for review instead of scrambling to post daily.
- [ ] Prepare eight short posts over two weeks. Each post should answer one real customer question or help the customer picture wearing the item.
- [ ] Keep the footage natural and simple, use a straightforward location and light, show the actual product clearly, and avoid heavy effects or overproduction.
- [ ] Reuse approved footage for Instagram Reels and TikTok; adjust the edit for each platform where it helps.
- [ ] Submit concepts, scripts, edits, captions, and finished posts to Sathvik for approval before publishing.
- [ ] Do not buy ads, spend money, promise discounts or shipping offers, or use artificial scarcity.

## 4. Make ASIOR’s point of view recognizable

- [ ] Ask Sathvik what actually inspired the ASIOR name, designs, and small-run approach.
- [ ] Use those real answers to explain what the brand represents; do not invent a deeper story just because storytelling can perform well.
- [ ] Test natural founder-led clips: a short explanation of why a piece was made, a design choice, a sample, or what Sathvik would improve after a release.
- [ ] Keep the founder’s delivery conversational rather than turning it into a stiff sales pitch.
- [ ] Let the product and the real people behind it carry the story; do not use a generic montage as a substitute for useful product information.

## 5. Explore creator and community collaborations carefully

- [ ] Start with existing contacts and Ayush’s outreach work.
- [ ] Ask photographers or fashion creators whether they are interested in a collaboration.
- [ ] Get Sathvik’s approval before promising free products, payment, or any other commitment.
- [ ] Disclose a creator relationship appropriately whenever the creator receives anything of value.
- [ ] Treat a collaboration as a test of fit with the brand, not a reason to spend or overpromise.

## 6. Measure learning, not just views

- [ ] Request a fresh weekly Shopify export of the same funnel measures and current stock totals.
- [ ] Where available, separate mobile and desktop results, inspect checkout errors, and track sessions from each published link.
- [ ] For every post, record its date, the question it answers, the format, engagement, clicks or sessions where available, and any corresponding store-result change.
- [ ] Treat early changes as signals, not proof that a specific post caused a sale; traffic is too low for confident attribution.
- [ ] Do not claim access to Shopify or Klaviyo data unless access has actually been granted.
- [ ] Keep the historical September 27 baseline separate from fresh weekly results.

| Measure | Historical baseline / plan target | Check |

| Sell-through goal | Roughly halve the earlier 21-week timeframe | [ ] Track progress toward roughly 10–11 weeks; do not present the workbook’s eight-week stretch scenario as the agreed goal. |
| Content performance | No consistent test baseline supplied | [ ] Learn which useful format earns attention and store visits; do not judge by views alone. |

## 7. Keep these approval and accuracy rules in force

- [ ] Get Sathvik’s approval for every outward-facing post, email, offer, and customer-facing statement.
- [ ] Do not spend money, run ads, promise shipping promotions, or create artificial scarcity.
- [ ] Do not publish stale September metrics as current performance.
- [ ] Do not invent materials, sizing, fit details, customer reviews, or reasons customers abandoned checkout.
- [ ] Do not send customer email or publish review requests without Sathvik’s approval.
- [ ] Confirm filming, creator participation, and collaboration commitments with Sathvik.

## Research references in the plan

1. Marshall Crews, [Marketing Your Clothing Brand is Simple, Actually (FREE COURSE)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8tMm_XZA0HU) — customer and brand identity before treating social media as a product catalog.
2. Rob Prsa, [Market Your Clothing Brand SO Good It Breaks the Internet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sE4KI56yGrU) — product-market fit, website quality, and social proof.
3. Rob Prsa, [Your 30 Day Plan to Blow Up Your Clothing Brand in 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YzTg8c5DnWI) — repeatable planning and batching; the plan excludes the paid-ad advice.
4. Marshall Crews, [The NEW Way to Build a WINNING Clothing Brand in 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ZNbjcKRWN4) — transparency and a real founder point of view.
5. Learn With Shopify, [How to Start A Clothing Brand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_p0eZTk8jKA) — background on brand identity, pricing, storefront quality, and organic marketing.
6. Marshall Crews, [I Stole Supreme’s Marketing Strategy And Made $100K With My Clothing Brand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9CTigCTKGDE) — streetwear positioning; use identity/community lessons, not unverifiable scarcity tactics.
7. Baymard Institute, [How to Reduce Cart Abandonment](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment) — hypotheses about checkout friction, not proof of ASIOR’s specific issue.
8. TikTok for Business, [2025 Fashion Playbook for Small Businesses](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-GB/blog/2025-tiktok-fashion-playbook) — fashion storytelling and product-led creative.
9. Federal Trade Commission, [Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) — boundaries for reviews and creator endorsements.
10. Shopify, [How to Get Product Reviews](https://www.shopify.com/blog/how-to-get-product-reviews) — timing and wording for genuine post-delivery review requests.
